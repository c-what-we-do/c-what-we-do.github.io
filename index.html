Manifesto

Today is a strange age full of bloatware, pooly designed tools, and lack of self-awareness about one's code. Sometimes, programmers simply feel tired of the mess. Sometimes, programmers just want to program purely to learn, tinker around, and deal with the "nitty-gritty". There are solutions to this, but here is ours:

Write C. Seriously, just write C.

It is the simple, minimalist, timeless classic of systems languages.

It does not get in your way or hide many things behind its back versus other system languages. It is very frank and straightforward with you, and that transparency is something to respect. Programmers should not just sweep technical details under the rug, but they should manage them with meticulous thought.

It is familiar in its syntax, having influenced other languages like JS, Java, and C++. Remember those familiar semicolons? Remember those braces? Remember types first in declarations? Every programmer knows of these features.

It practically has a compiler for every known architecture and a super stable ABI... It is extremely portable and reliable. That is why C will remain as good old C.

— DrkWithT
